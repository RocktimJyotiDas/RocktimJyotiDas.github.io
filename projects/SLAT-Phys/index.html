Abstract

Estimating spatially varying material properties from visual observations is important for physics-based simulation and robotics, but existing approaches often require multi-view observations, an existing 3D asset, or expensive per-object representation construction. We present SLAT-Phys, a feedforward framework that predicts material property fields from a single RGB image by reusing structured 3D latents from a pretrained image-to-3D model. A lightweight sparse transformer operates directly on these latents to estimate Young’s modulus (E), density (ρ), and Poisson’s ratio (ν) without constructing a separate 3D feature field.

Under the Pixie evaluation protocol, SLAT-Phys outperforms prior methods on most reported continuous-property metrics while remaining competitive in material classification. The complete pipeline requires approximately 9.9 seconds per object on an NVIDIA RTX A5000 GPU, up to 184× faster than prior methods. These results demonstrate that pretrained structured 3D latents provide an effective and efficient representation for single-image material-property prediction.

Method

How it works

1

SLAT Feature Extraction

A single RGB image is encoded by the frozen SAM-3D-Objects image-to-3D model into Structured LATent (SLAT) features — sparse occupied surface-voxel coordinates with 8-dimensional latent vectors on a 643 grid.

2

Physics Decoder

A lightweight sparse Swin-style transformer (256 channels, 8 blocks, 16 heads) maps SLAT features directly to per-voxel Young’s modulus, density, and Poisson’s ratio, plus one of eight material classes — no separate 3D feature field is constructed.

3

Surface-to-Volume Extrapolation

The same SLAT is decoded into 3D Gaussians. Following PhysGaussian’s internal filling, interior voxels are found by ray-parity tests and inherit the material parameters of their nearest predicted surface voxel, yielding a dense volumetric material field.

4

MPM Physics Simulation

The volumetric material field and decoded Gaussian geometry are passed to a Material Point Method (MPM) solver to simulate physically plausible deformation under external forces.

Key Contributions

  • Feedforward material fields from one image. SLAT-Phys predicts continuous, spatially varying material property fields from a single image by operating directly on structured 3D latents, with no multi-view capture, existing 3D asset, or per-object feature field.
  • Structured latents are physics-informative. Spatially organized SLAT features let a lightweight decoder estimate Young’s modulus, density, and Poisson’s ratio accurately.
  • Accurate, fast, and useful downstream. SLAT-Phys beats reconstruction-based methods on most continuous-property metrics with up to 184× faster inference, and provides physics annotations for downstream robotic simulation.
